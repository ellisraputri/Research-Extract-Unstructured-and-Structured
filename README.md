## Abstract
The healthcare field has always been heavily reliant on the volume and quality of data. With the increase in edge devices capable of acquiring data ubiquitously, the volume of data is increasing rapidly while compromising its quality. Most of the data acquired through edge devices in the medical field or for intended medical advice is in an unstructured format, which causes an information overload. Therefore, it is essential to transform the data into a structured form first. The transformation from unstructured to structured depends on various factors that affect the generalizing ability of the method to be used across data types. Thanks to the advancement in large language models (LLMs), they can be used naturally for the aforementioned transformation. In this work, we use the data from Rumah Sakit Gunung Jati in Cirebon. We show that the use of LLMs such as Gemini 2.0-Flash helps achieve a generalization for the transformation task while helping the classification accuracy to improve by 1.7% on average across 10 different classifiers. We also conduct statistical tests to show that the improvement recorded through LLMs is significant, i.e., p = 0.00636. Lastly, we provide future directions by extending the analysis to ensemble-based models accordingly.
